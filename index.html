About

I am a researcher specializing in first-principles electronic structure theory, with a focus on quantum light–matter interactions and spectroscopy.

My current research centers on Quantum Electrodynamical Density Functional Theory (QEDFT) and time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) to investigate quantum light–matter interactions at molecule–metal interfaces. I study how strong coupling between photons and electrons modifies the electronic structure, optical response, and excited-state properties of these hybrid systems.

A major application of my research is the development of theoretical approaches to understand and enhance Surface-Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (SERS) and Tip-Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (TERS) by exploring the interplay between plasmonic nanostructures, molecular excitations, and quantum light–matter interactions.

Previously, I worked on the Chiral-Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS) effect, investigating spin-dependent electron transport in chiral molecular systems in a molecular junction setup. I did my PhD focussing on singlet fission, studying excitonic processes relevant to next-generation photovoltaic materials using density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) method.

My research interests include QEDFT, DFT, TDDFT, quantum light–matter interactions, Raman spectroscopy, plasmonics, molecule–metal interfaces, excited-state dynamics, and spin-dependent phenomena.
